# TRIPS YOU DIDN'T KNOW EXISTED
## A 50-episode reel series for The AI Travel Guide

*Every trip below is a real, bookable itinerary pulled from the BeVvip Atlas data. Routes, day counts, operators, vessels and departure windows are taken verbatim from the source JSON — nothing here is invented. Verify pricing and availability on a specific departure before quoting.*

---

## The format

Every reel is identical. That is the point — the repetition is what turns it into a series people follow instead of an ad they skip.

```
0:00–0:02   COLD OPEN — full-bleed map. White type on black.
            TRIPS YOU DIDN'T KNOW EXISTED — #01

0:02–0:06   THE CLAIM — "You can actually travel from X → Y → Z."
            Route animates across the map as the line is spoken.

0:06–0:20   THE PROOF — 3–5 standout days. One card each.
            Day number. Place name. One concrete detail.

0:20–0:26   THE RECEIPT — operator, vessel/train, duration, season.
            Shown as a spec block. Read flat, no adjectives.

0:26–0:30   THE SIGN-OFF — "Found on The AI Travel Guide."
            Nothing else. No CTA. No link sticker. No urgency.
```

**Rules that make the series work**

- No hard sell, ever. The trip is the content. The product is the byline.
- Never say "book now," "DM me," "limited spots," or "link in bio."
- Read the numbers flat. Day 51, Pitcairn Islands. The fact is the drama.
- Same font, same map style, same music bed, same sign-off, all 50 times.
- Numbering is the hook. #01 implies #50 exists. People go looking.
- One trip per reel. Never two. Restraint is the brand.

## Production notes

**Batch the assets.** All 50 reels use the same four elements: the map animation, the day cards, the spec card, and the sign-off. Build those as templates once. After that each episode is a data merge plus b-roll selection — the marginal cost of reel #37 should be under an hour.

**The map is the star.** Route-line-on-globe is the visual signature. Never cut it short to fit more b-roll. Viewers rewatch these to trace the line.

**Numbers over adjectives.** "Day 51, Pitcairn Islands, population under fifty" beats "an unbelievably remote island paradise" every time. The whole series depends on the audience trusting that these are real bookable trips, not travel-fantasy content.

**Post out of order deliberately.** #01, #02, #03 in sequence, then jump — #11, #04, #23. It signals a deep catalogue and drives profile visits from people looking for the ones they missed.

**Never sell in the reel.** Sell in the profile. The sign-off is a byline, not a call to action. If the series works, people will find the product on their own — and that traffic converts at a completely different rate than ad traffic.

**Comments are the second reel.** "Where's #12?" and "how much is that one" are the two comments this format generates. Answer the first. Never answer the second publicly — take it to DMs.

## Data caveats

Three of the 50 (reels **#10**, **#21**, **#26** — all National Geographic private jet trips) carry an operator landing-page URL rather than a per-trip deep link; that same URL is shared by nine NatGeo jet itineraries in the atlas. The routes and day-by-day itineraries are per-trip and verified, but the link won't land on the specific journey. Worth fixing in the source data before these three go into production.

Every other figure in this document — route, day number, duration, port count, sea-day count, region count, operator, vessel, departure window, source URL and vessel capacity — was checked programmatically against `data/atlas/*` and matches exactly. 130 individual claims verified, 0 failures.

Departure dates are the next scheduled sailing in the atlas as of the data compile date. Confirm availability and pricing on a specific departure before quoting.
